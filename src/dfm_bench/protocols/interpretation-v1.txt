Additional v1 interpretation rules, developed by AI role reviewers; not human gold:
1. Importance measures the consequence of resolving the question, independently of positive/negative direction or completed execution. A rigorous negative result or infeasibility closure can be consequential; quantify its scope. Do not treat small or null predictive gains alone as low scientific importance.
2. Novelty is relative to supplied sources only. Lack of retrieved matches is not evidence of originality. Keep global novelty unassessed.
3. Ask whether a proposed derived predictor is mathematically determined by comparator inputs. Added predictive information beyond those same inputs requires a distinct parameterization, interaction or estimator claim; generic complexity does not create new information.
4. Separate design specificity, feasibility diagnostics, executed outcome tests and independent confirmation. Outcome-masked feasibility can support a design but cannot establish predictive/clinical/material utility or physical mechanism.
5. Read control experiments precisely: destroying value-label correspondence under within-day reassignment does not by itself prove panel identity is the source of gain. Compare the actual operational control and its result.
6. Preserve independent units: patient/site and informative clinical observation, versus cell/specimen/condition and shared cycling history. More rows or spectra do not imply more independent units. Censoring/source semantics and unavailable future measurements constrain resolvability.
7. Missing literature or endpoint evidence remains unknown when genuinely unassessable. A plainly underspecified test is an observed weakness, not automatically unknown. Do not convert an engineering completion into scientific merit.
8. Preserve contradictions and data-source provenance. Where a supplied digest is corrected by a hash-verified source erratum, use the correction; do not punish the hypothesis for a summarizer error.
Apply the original eight dimensions and weights unchanged; do not target a desired score, order, trend, or amount of improvement.
